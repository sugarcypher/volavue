THE FRAMEWORK

Personal Decision Topology

This work is grounded in the Personal Decision Topology framework. We examine five structural dimensions.

Forces at Play

This dimension identifies motivations, incentives, fears, and constraints shaping the behavior of the people involved. It clarifies what is driving movement in the situation, whether stated openly or operating in the background.

Relational Dynamics

This dimension examines the structure between people: dependencies, asymmetries, obligations, alignments, and recurring patterns. It helps show where leverage, friction, or distortion may be entering the decision environment.

Active Pressures

This dimension looks at present-tense pressures affecting judgment and timing. These can include financial strain, deadlines, social expectations, emotional intensity, or external demands that compress the decision space.

Underlying Structure

This dimension focuses on what is not immediately visible but materially influential. It may include unspoken incentives, hidden conflicts, identity tensions, or systemic arrangements that shape outcomes beneath the surface.

Probable Consequence Paths

This dimension models how different choices are likely to unfold over time. It is used to clarify second-order effects, irreversible thresholds, and feedback loops before action is taken.
